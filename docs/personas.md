## Persona 1: The Stressed Loser

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Amina Chebet |
| **Age / Year of study** | 20, Second year |
| **Typical scenario** | Lost her water bottle near the library; on a previous occasion lost a USB drive with coursework on it |
| **Tech comfort** | Comfortable with everyday apps, checks her phone frequently when anxious |

**Background**
Amina realizes she's lost something shortly after leaving a location and immediately starts worrying about whether it's gone for good. She's not sure whether reporting it will actually help, or whether anyone finds and returns things on this campus.

**Goals**
- Report a lost item quickly, before the details (time, location) get fuzzy in her memory
- Feel reassured that reporting actually leads somewhere, not just into a void
- Know as soon as possible if someone found her item
- Trust that if she does claim an item, the verification process actually confirms it's really hers (and isn't just first-come-first-served)

**Frustrations** *(working assumptions — validate against real research)*
- Doesn't know if reporting a lost item does anything without some kind of confirmation or tracking
- Anxious about a "finders keepers" risk — worried someone else could falsely claim her item, or that she couldn't prove an item is hers
- Doesn't want to fill out a long form while stressed and in a hurry between classes

**How this app helps her**
- Report Lost Item (Screen 3) is a short, focused form — name, description, location, date, category — not an exhaustive intake form
- Confirmation screen (Screen 5) with a report ID gives her something concrete, reducing the "did this even work?" anxiety
- Verifying Your Claim (Screen 10) — comparing finder's description to claimant's — directly addresses her fear of false claims
- Claim Status Tracker (Screen 11) lets her check progress without having to ask anyone

## Persona 2: The Helpful Finder

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | David Mwangi |
| **Age / Year of study** | 22, Final year |
| **Typical scenario** | Found a student ID card in the cafeteria; wants to report it but isn't going to go out of his way for it |
| **Tech comfort** | High — uses several apps daily, low patience for friction |

**Background**
David found something that clearly isn't his and would like to do the right thing, but he's busy and won't put in significant effort. If reporting a found item feels like a chore, he's more likely to just leave it at a front desk or not bother at all.

**Goals**
- Report a found item in under a minute, with minimal typing
- Not be responsible for holding onto the item indefinitely or chasing up the owner himself
- Feel like reporting actually connects the item to its owner, so his effort wasn't wasted

**Frustrations** *(working assumptions — validate against real research)*
- Low tolerance for a long or detailed form (photo upload + identifier tags could feel like too much effort if not clearly quick)
- Doesn't want to manage direct contact with a stranger to hand off an item
- Uncertain what to do with the item physically after reporting it (where does it go?)

**Quote** *(placeholder — replace with a real participant quote once research is conducted)*
> "[Replace with a real quote once collected]"

**How this app helps him**
- Report Found Item (Screen 4) — photo + identifier tags — should be framed as quick, visual, low-effort rather than a detailed intake form
- Contact preference field lets him control how much direct interaction he has with the claimant
- Drop-off Location (Screen 12) gives a clear, campus-wide answer to "where does the item actually go," so he isn't stuck holding it or guessing
